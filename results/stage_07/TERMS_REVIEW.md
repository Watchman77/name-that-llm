# Source/use review — 18 October 2026

Scope: existing publicly distributed responses for an authorship classifier. No provider calls, API subscription, new generation, communication with authors, model-weight distribution or competition submission was made. These are source-document findings and bounded working interpretations, not an assertion that every private collection agreement has been inspected.

## Findings from primary documents

- SpeechMap repository: Apache-2.0 licence independently read at commit `476ea8f0adf85b737bbb6e38e838448a8d4f2a9b`. This establishes the repository's declared licence; it does not authenticate all upstream rights. Source: https://github.com/xlr8harder/speechmap-data/blob/476ea8f0adf85b737bbb6e38e838448a8d4f2a9b/LICENSE . The mixed corpus's `output_terms_review` flag is a review request, not a finding that classification training is prohibited.
- OpenRouter terms (page last updated August 31, 2026): sections 5 and 6 defer model access/use and output ownership to applicable model terms. Router availability or public publication does not waive those terms. Source: https://openrouter.ai/terms . Current distillable-text routing documentation describes an optional author-permission filter; existing logs do not establish that it was enabled. Do not infer a blanket refusal of classifier use from its absence. Source: https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/routing/provider-selection . No OpenRouter inference was performed in this review.
- OpenAI Services Agreement: section 3.3(e) restricts competing-model development subject to a Permitted Exception. Its definitions explicitly include models primarily intended to categorize/classify/organize data when not distributed or made commercially available to third parties. Section 4 assigns output rights to the customer as between the parties. Source: https://openai.com/policies/services-agreement/ . A private, undistributed authorship classifier is a plausible fit for that exception; this does not establish permission to distribute trained weights or that every source agreement used these exact terms. Competition deployment/distribution needs separate assessment.
- Anthropic commercial terms, sections B and D.4: customer output ownership is accompanied by restrictions on accessing the service to build competing products, including competing AI models. Source: https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms . An attribution classifier is not automatically a competing generative model. That is a working interpretation of the proposed task, not an explicit published approval for this particular corpus. SpeechMap also has Bedrock-served rows; do not claim the direct Anthropic agreement is necessarily their collection contract.
- Google Developer API terms restrict competing-model development and do not assert Google's ownership of newly generated content. Source: https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms . **These are not sufficient to review a Vertex route.** For Vertex/Cloud, the Cloud Service Specific Terms sections on AI/ML restrictions and Generative AI output are relevant: they restrict competing/similar model uses and treat Generated Output as Customer Data without Google asserting ownership in new output IP. Source: https://cloud.google.com/terms/service-terms . We read the Cloud document too; no claim of a blanket ban on every classifier follows from the competing-model language. The collector's actual agreement, model/version and route still matter.

## Dataset declarations and admission boundary

Oleg's V1 is explicitly a mixed-source collection, not a blanket Apache relicensing. Haileycheng's V2 and prvsiyan's proxy-only V1 both disclose that Kaggle's upstream proxy agreements are not public. Do not convert their CC declarations into independent verification of those agreements.

The more promising candidate, `prvsiyan/prvsiyans-extended-name-that-llm-dataset` V4, declares CC BY 4.0 and publishes requested/served model IDs, route and raw response envelopes. Its selected `new` subset still contains 524 proxy rows among 4,068 rows; proxy echoes are weaker identity evidence than a provider-side version field. Its other routes include first-party Anthropic/OpenAI/Google and OpenRouter. The current review did not establish all private proxy agreements or all Moonshot/Alibaba/Z.AI collection terms. Consequently the review does not grant unconditional all-route training/distribution approval.

The important correction to the first audit is that covering only a subset of external classes is not itself a blocker: an augmentation pilot can still train and evaluate all 18 official classes with fixed external weights and explicit class counts. Conversely, an all-18 headline is not an approval or a quality guarantee.

## Decision

Do not spend time resolving every SpeechMap collection contract merely to run the bulk augmentation trial: its measured domain mismatch already makes it a lower-priority first candidate. This is a prioritization decision, not an observed model failure or a legal prohibition.

Review the extended V4 candidate route by route before admitting rows. A narrow private classifier experiment and a distributed competition artifact are different uses. Keep any proposed generative fine-tune outside this use assessment: a private classifier exception must not be silently extended to it. No request for the user's permission is being substituted for missing evidence.
